## 8. LinkedIn distribution system

```
Website     = canonical knowledge base (the argument, in full, forever)
LinkedIn    = distribution + conversation (compressed fragments, pointing back)
Projects    = evidence (what was actually built)
Books       = intellectual assets (owned, long-form, already real)
Resume      = credibility (career record, verifiable)
Contact     = conversion (where a real conversation starts)
```

Publishing loop (same shape as Phase 14's launch workflow, restated at the strategic level):

```
IDEA → ARTICLE → LINKEDIN → DISCUSSION → INSIGHT → FOLLOW-UP CONTENT
```

**Realistic cadence for one person:** roughly one new long-form article every 3–6 weeks, each
distributed across its own 1–2 week LinkedIn window (Day 0/1/3/5/7 pattern already documented in
Phase 14), rather than a constant content machine. A sustainable rhythm beats a burst that can't be
maintained — three real articles in reasonable time with real distribution beats ten rushed ones
with none.

## 14. Success criteria (not vanity metrics)

Explicitly **not** measured by: page count, schema object count, backlink count, or article count
alone. Measured instead by:

- A consistent professional identity across the site and LinkedIn (qualitative, checkable).
- Increasing branded search visibility over time (Search Console, watched not predicted).
- Original content actually indexed (a real, binary Search Console fact per page).
- Real LinkedIn engagement specifically around the core ideas (comments/discussion, not just
  reactions).
- Actual professional conversations that trace back to a piece of content.
- Genuine external mentions, if and when they occur.
- Real invitations, interviews, or collaborations, if and when they occur.
- Any real reference to one of the original frameworks by someone else.
- Qualified professional contact through `/contact`, tied to real interest in the actual work.
- A strengthening, observable association between "Milad Ebrahimi" and "Business Operations /
  Systems" specifically — the one outcome every other item on this list is really a proxy for.

## Risks / anti-patterns to actively avoid

- Treating architecture (schema, sitemap, structured data) as if it were authority — it's the
  precondition, not the result.
- Publishing content faster than it can be genuinely distributed and discussed — quality and real
  engagement over volume.
- Chasing the AI/Productivity content gap with a rushed article instead of the better-evidenced
  Marketing gap.
- Any of the excluded tactics in §9 — all of them create a signal that looks like authority
  without being real, and the moment it's discovered (by a person or by a platform), it costs more
  credibility than it ever bought.
- Rewriting older LinkedIn/CV history to look more consistent with the current positioning — real,
  evolving career history is more credible than a retroactively cleaned one.
