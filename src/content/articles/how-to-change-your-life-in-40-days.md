What if you could change your life in forty days? Not with motivation, but with a simple system you
run every morning and every night.

This system comes from my book, *Self-Coaching Blueprint*. Below is the whole thing, step by step:
why change fails at the start, where you are, what you value, what you want, a forty-day goal, and
the daily routine that makes it stick. The full video is at the end of this page.

## 1. Why change fails at the start

When you start something new, your mind quietly puts up three barriers. Their job is simple: keep you
where you are, in your comfort zone (p. 22).

- **A superficial view.** You read something new, think it seems interesting, and move on, without
  giving it the attention it deserves.
- **A negative view.** "We've read so many books already. None of them changed our lives."
- **Inaction.** Even when it's time to act, you hesitate. "Come on, who has the energy for all this?"

And when a plan doesn't work perfectly right away, avoid the trap of saying, "See? I knew it wouldn't
work." That's simply not the case (p. 21).

![Diagram: you, in your comfort zone, then three barriers in a row (a superficial view, a negative view, inaction), then change. Awareness plus committed action brings them down.](/images/articles/forty-days/barriers.webp)


These are old habits of the mind. Once you become aware of them and take committed action, everything
can change. Don't expect sudden change, though. The goal is not perfection. It's improvement (p. 21).

That's why I ask for forty days. For the next forty days, give your full attention to this process.
These days are pivotal (p. 23).

## 2. Find out where you are

In the book I use the Balance Wheel. It covers eight areas of life: business, finance, lifestyle,
health, relationships, social, mental and spiritual. It's a common method used by business consultants
and coaches: assessment through scoring (p. 49).

Score each area from 1 to 10. There are no wrong answers and no negative scores. The only requirement
is honesty, and no one else will see your answers. Then connect the points on the wheel. If you see
flat spots, you'll understand why your life may not be turning as smoothly as you'd like.

![Example Balance Wheel with eight scores from 1 to 10: business 7, finance 5, lifestyle 6, health 8, relationships 3, social 6, mental 7, spiritual 5. Relationships is the flat spot. Beside it, the three patterns: balance, intention, aggression.](/images/articles/forty-days/balance-wheel.webp)


You can do this right now with the [online Balance Wheel](/balance-wheel/), or print the
[free two-page worksheet](/start/).

Then choose where to focus. Three patterns help (p. 67):

- **Balance:** which area has the lowest score, and do you feel most motivated to improve it?
- **Intention:** which area do you want to dedicate your time and effort to over the next forty days?
- **Aggression:** which one is most crucial to improve right now?

From all the areas, choose about three to focus on over the next forty days (p. 68). And remember,
balance is personal. It's not about other people's standards. It's about defining what balance means
to you (p. 33).

## 3. Discover what you value

Think of the people you admire. Write down at least five names. The first names that come to mind are
often the most significant.

Now choose three. Under each name, list at least ten qualities you admire about them. Mark the traits
they share, and write down the three most essential ones. Ask yourself: which qualities set them apart
from others, and which ones do I envy them for possessing (p. 41)?

![Diagram: five people you admire, narrowed to three, ten qualities listed under each, and finally three core values.](/images/articles/forty-days/values.webp)


Here's why this matters. The characteristics you admire in others are often the same traits you wish to
cultivate within yourself. These qualities form the foundation of your value system (p. 42). And they
directly influence your daily decisions: whether intentionally or not, you tend to choose goals that let
you experience these traits in your own life.

## 4. Decide what you want

One of the main drivers of our decisions is the desire to avoid pain and seek pleasure. So let's use it
(p. 75).

Draw two columns: **pain** and **pleasure**. Under pain, write what you don't want in your life. What
causes you discomfort? What are you trying to distance yourself from? Then, for each one, ask: what would
I like to experience instead? Write it under pleasure.

Now cross out the pain. From now on, direct all your attention and energy toward the alternative. The
clearer and more specific your goals are, the faster you can achieve them.

![Two columns. Pain, crossed out: rushing through every morning, ending the day exhausted, worrying about money each month. Pleasure: calm planned mornings, energy left for the evening, a clear plan for my income. Examples only.](/images/articles/forty-days/pain-pleasure.webp)


If you have more than three goals, think of them as one bowl (p. 77). Say your main goal is to increase
your income. The bowl could hold smaller goals: friends with higher incomes who inspire you, less time on
social media, managing your time better, and specific actions that directly grow your income.

![A bowl with the main goal "increase my income" on top and four smaller goals inside: friends with higher incomes who inspire you, less time on social media, managing your time better, specific actions that grow your income.](/images/articles/forty-days/goal-bowl.webp)


## 5. Turn it into a forty-day goal

Don't overwhelm yourself. Choose three main goals, and give them forty days or less. When you reach a
short-term goal, you build confidence in the process, and that makes your bigger, long-term goals easier
and faster. Start with what you can control. Progress there will motivate you to tackle bigger
challenges (p. 78).

Next, break each goal into **micro-actions**. Ask yourself: what do I need to do every day to achieve
this goal? An example from the book: the goal is to lose six kilos in forty days, and the micro-action is
to walk thirty minutes about five days a week. That's twenty-five walks.

![A forty-day calendar for the goal "lose 6 kg in 40 days": a walk symbol on five days of each week, twenty-five walks in all.](/images/articles/forty-days/forty-days.webp)


Give each action its own symbol. You'll see it every day on your daily balance page, and it keeps the
action in front of you. The exact timing isn't what matters most. What matters is that you've set a
timeline and you're moving steadily toward it (p. 80).

Be patient. As the saying goes, even if a gardener floods his garden, the trees will not bear fruit out
of season. And don't cling to your goals: don't sacrifice everything for one goal, like neglecting your
body for money, or working so much that you forget your family. A true human being is a balanced human
being (p. 81).

## 6. Every morning: three important tasks

For the next forty days, complete a short **morning balance** as soon as you wake up (pp. 89–91).

1. Write what you'd like to experience today. Don't worry about how it will happen.
2. Choose your focus: what would I like to focus on most today?
3. Ask: if I could only complete three tasks today, which three would have the biggest impact? Write
   those three first.

Everything else, like buying groceries or transferring money, goes on your regular to-do list.
Necessary, but it doesn't directly move you forward.

![A morning balance page: what I'd like to experience today, my focus today, and three numbered lines for the three tasks with the biggest impact. Beside it, a faded regular to-do list: buy groceries, transfer money.](/images/articles/forty-days/morning.webp)


## 7. Every night: two questions

Before bed, ask yourself two questions (p. 93):

1. What score would I give myself today, from 1 to 10?
2. How can I get a better score tomorrow? Find one small step, and decide on it tonight.

Some tasks will stay unfinished. Move the essential ones to tomorrow, and drop anything that's no longer
relevant (p. 94). Then write what you're thankful for today, something small or significant. It sets the
tone for a more energized tomorrow (p. 96).

To score your day, the book uses a simple ten-point template (p. 97):

![The two night questions and the ten-point day: 1 point for the morning balance, 1 for the night balance, 3 for the three important tasks, 3 for three improvement actions, 2 for how you feel. Below 3 is minimal effort, 3 to 6 commendable, 6 to 10 on the path to progress.](/images/articles/forty-days/night-score.webp)

Below 3 means your effort to change has been minimal. From 3 to 6 is commendable. From 6 to 10 means
you're on the path to progress (p. 98). Over time, you'll live with these questions, moment by moment,
wherever you are.

## Your forty days

That's the system. Notice the barriers. Find your balance. Know your values. Decide what you want. Give
it forty days. And run it every morning and every night.

Change doesn't happen in an instant. Find joy in the journey, and let time guide you (p. 96).
