context: >
  Engineers across several active project sites were reporting daily progress informally, with no
  consistent structure for what got captured or how it reached management.
problem: >
  Daily activity on site wasn't being captured in a form that could reliably feed weekly or
  monthly reporting — so reporting further up the chain depended on reconstructing what happened
  after the fact.
challenge: >
  Keeping the report simple enough that a site engineer would actually complete it every day,
  while making it structured enough to support a proper review process before anything reached
  management.
approach: >
  Designed a short daily report — work completed, plan for tomorrow, workers on site, materials
  needed, issues — paired with a simple compliance rule: at least one site photo shared in the
  project's WhatsApp group as visual confirmation.
system: >
  A daily reporting workflow built on tools the site teams already used, with a defined review
  path before a report becomes part of the official record.
howItWorks: >
  An engineer submits the daily report and it's logged automatically. For higher-stakes reporting
  contexts, submissions pass through a technical review step before approval, and only approved
  reports become eligible for management-level reporting.
tools: ["Google Forms", "Google Sheets", "WhatsApp", "Google Drive"]
implementation: >
  In active use across multiple project sites, with weeks of real submissions from site
  engineers. A more structured version of the same workflow — including a formal technical-review
  and approval chain — has also been designed as a reference build for a future, more integrated
  reporting system.
evidenceNote: >
  No formal adoption or time-saved metric exists yet — that evidence is still in development.
