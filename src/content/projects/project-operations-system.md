context: >
  The operations function touched site execution, materials, finance, and cross-department
  coordination — but without one documented rhythm, consistency depended on memory and habit.
problem: >
  There was no single reference for what the operations role owned each day, what decisions it
  could make alone, and when something needed to be escalated.
challenge: >
  Covering a genuinely wide scope — site execution, procurement, finance coordination,
  cross-department communication — in one system precise enough to actually be followed daily.
approach: >
  Documented the role as a literal hour-by-hour schedule rather than a general job description,
  paired with a clear decision-authority matrix so escalation stopped being a judgment call.
system: >
  A daily operating system for the role: an hour-by-hour schedule, a one-page job description, a
  decision-authority matrix, and a defined set of tracking instruments — a daily report log, a
  project progress tracker, a cost tracker, and an issue log.
howItWorks: >
  The day runs on a fixed structure — site reports reviewed each morning, materials and
  procurement reviewed mid-morning, finance coordination before noon, cross-department check-ins
  at midday, site follow-up in the afternoon, and reporting closed out by end of day — with a
  matching weekly rhythm for planning and review.
tools: ["Google Sheets", "WhatsApp"]
implementation: >
  In active use as the standing operating rhythm for the role.
evidenceNote: >
  This is a process and accountability system rather than one with its own standalone
  before/after metric — its effect shows up in the consistency of the systems it coordinates,
  including daily site reporting and material request tracking.
